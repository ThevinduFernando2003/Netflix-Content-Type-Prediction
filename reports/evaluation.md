## Three tracks

The leaky baseline includes `duration_unit`. The honest model does not. The no-TV-vocabulary model is the honest model with a fixed 20-word format stoplist removed from the title before TF-IDF. This extract has no synopsis, so the ablation is on the title. The gap between leaky and honest is the value of the banned shortcut. The gap between honest and no-TV-vocabulary is how much of the remaining score is the word `series`, `movie`, or `season` rather than the rest of the title.

Of the 25 most confident mistakes, 20 are TV shows scored as movies and 5 are movies scored as TV shows.
80% of them carry a banned TV-format genre token in `listed_in`. The honest model never sees that token, so a series whose title reads like a film title is scored from country, rating, year, and title wording alone.
76% have no director credit. That missingness is a strong catalogue cue and it was banned on purpose, which is why several series with a blank director credit are still called movies.
20% have a title of one or two words, which leaves the TF-IDF block with almost no signal. The source file also has no synopsis, so there is no second text field to recover from. The recurring pattern is catalogue convention (genre name, missing director) that the honest model refuses to use, plus short titles that do not say whether the work is a film or a series.
